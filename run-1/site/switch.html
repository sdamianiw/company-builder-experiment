Inventora users

If you are on Inventora and have experienced inventory inaccuracies, overselling during sales, or slow support response, BatchCOGS may be a more reliable fit. Inventora's Shopify App Store rating is 3.9/5 across 8 reviews, with documented sync failures and duplication issues (Shopify App Store). BatchCOGS uses idempotency keys on every webhook to prevent double-deductions and publishes a real-time status page.

If Inventora has been working without errors for your operation, the switching cost (re-entering your materials and recipes) may not be worth a $4/month saving over Inventora's Shopify App Store price of $23/month. The free tier of BatchCOGS lets you test the integration before committing.

Step-by-step export

Export your data before you cancel

Cancel your subscription only after you have confirmed your exports are complete and imported into BatchCOGS. Choose your current platform below.

Craftybase rebranded to Stocksmith in July 2026. The craftybase.com domain now redirects to stocksmith.io with no legacy content. The export steps below apply to both the old Craftybase interface and the current Stocksmith interface. Source: stocksmith.io.

01

List every material in your library before exporting

Open your materials list and note how many active materials you have. This number is your post-import verification target — after you import into BatchCOGS, confirm the count matches.

02

Export your materials / supplies as CSV

In Stocksmith, go to Supplies (or Materials, depending on your account version) and use the Export or Download CSV option. The file will include supply name, unit of measure, cost per unit, current stock quantity, and supplier information.

Save this file with today's date in the filename so you have a dated snapshot (e.g., supplies-2026-07-23.csv).

03

Export your products and recipes / BOMs

In Stocksmith, export your Products list. Then export your Recipes or Bills of Materials — these are the links between products and the materials they consume. Most Craftybase/Stocksmith plans export these as separate CSV files.

If your plan does not include a recipe export option, photograph or screenshot each recipe manually before canceling. You will need the ingredient quantities per unit to re-enter them in BatchCOGS.

04

Export your production history

Download your Production runs or Manufacturing log if you use it. This data does not import into BatchCOGS directly, but you want a local copy before closing the account. Store it alongside your other exports.

05

Record your current stock levels for each material

BatchCOGS will not automatically know your opening stock — you enter it during setup. Note the current quantity on hand for each material. The supplies CSV you exported in step 02 should include this column; verify it is present before moving on.

06

Export your order history

Download your Sales or Orders history as CSV. BatchCOGS does not import historical orders, but this record belongs to you and you should have it before the account closes.

07

Set your subscription to cancel at period end — not immediately

If you cancel immediately, you lose access before the billing period ends. Set the cancellation to take effect at the end of your current billing period. This gives you read-only access to verify your exports are complete and nothing was missed.

08

Disconnect Etsy and Shopify from Stocksmith

Before you connect BatchCOGS to your shops, disconnect Stocksmith from Etsy and Shopify. Running two webhook listeners on the same shop can cause duplicate processing. Disconnect from Stocksmith first, then connect BatchCOGS.

Inventora is priced at $23/month on the Shopify App Store and $19/month on inventora.com (annual billing). Source: Shopify App Store and inventora.com/pricing-plans.

01

Export your components / materials

In Inventora, navigate to Components and export as CSV. The file includes component name, unit of measure, cost, and quantity on hand. This maps directly to the BatchCOGS materials library.

02

Export your products

Export your Products list from Inventora. This gives you a record of every SKU you sell. You will re-enter these in BatchCOGS and link them to materials via recipes.

03

Export your bills of materials

Export your Bills of Materials (BOM) from Inventora. This is the key file — it records how many units of each component go into each product. Without it, you will need to re-enter all recipe quantities by hand.

04

Verify your current stock quantities

If Inventora has had sync errors, your stock quantities in the system may not reflect actual stock on hand. Do a physical count for your highest-volume materials before treating any exported number as ground truth. Enter the physical count — not the Inventora figure — as your opening stock in BatchCOGS.

05

Export your vendor / supplier list

Download your Vendors list. This maps to the Supplier field in BatchCOGS materials. Having it exported saves re-typing supplier names during import.

06

Uninstall Inventora from Shopify before connecting BatchCOGS

Go to your Shopify admin, navigate to Apps, and uninstall Inventora. This removes Inventora's webhooks from your store. Confirm the uninstall completes before connecting BatchCOGS — overlapping webhook subscriptions cause the inventory duplication that Inventora reviewers have documented (Shopify App Store reviews).

07

Disconnect Inventora from Etsy

In your Etsy shop settings, revoke Inventora's API access under Apps and integrations. Then connect BatchCOGS to Etsy via its OAuth flow.

Field mapping

What maps to what

Use this reference while you prepare your import files. BatchCOGS field names are on the right; your source field is on the left.

Stocksmith / Craftybase → BatchCOGS

Stocksmith field BatchCOGS field Notes
Supply name / Material name → material Direct match
Unit of measure → unit Use the same abbreviation (oz, lb, ml, g, each)
Cost per unit → cost_per_unit Enter as a decimal (e.g., 2.40). BatchCOGS stores cost at the unit level you define.
Supplier / Vendor → supplier Optional — used for filtering and reorder reference
On hand (stock quantity) → Opening stock (set during material creation) Enter your verified physical count, not the exported system figure, if there is any doubt
Product / Listing title → Product name Match exactly to your Etsy or Shopify listing title so the webhook deduction auto-links
Recipe ingredient quantity → Quantity per unit (in recipe builder) The amount of each material consumed per one finished unit sold
Low stock threshold → Low-stock alert level Set during material creation or editing in BatchCOGS

Inventora → BatchCOGS

Inventora field BatchCOGS field Notes
Component name → material Direct match
Component unit → unit Keep units consistent across materials and recipes
Component cost → cost_per_unit Confirm this is cost per unit at the unit type you defined, not per case or per bulk order
Vendor → supplier Optional
Current stock → Opening stock If Inventora had sync issues, do a physical count before trusting this number
BOM component quantity → Quantity per unit (recipe builder) How many units of this material go into one finished product unit
Import templates

Download CSV templates

Two templates to fill from your exported data. The materials template goes first — you reference materials by name when building recipes in the second template.

Materials template

One row per material. Fill in name, unit of measure, cost per unit (decimal), and supplier. This is the foundation your recipes reference.

material, unit, cost_per_unit, supplier

CSV · 4 columns · sample rows included

Recipe template

One row per material per product. A product with three ingredients gets three rows. Material names must match exactly what you entered in your materials list.

product_sku, product_name, material, quantity_per_unit, unit

CSV · 5 columns · sample rows included

Import order matters. Add your materials first, then build your recipes. The recipe template references materials by name — if a material name in your recipe file does not match the name in your materials list exactly, BatchCOGS will flag it rather than create a silent mismatch.

After you import

Three steps after your data is in

01

Connect your shop

Connect Etsy, Shopify, or both via OAuth. BatchCOGS registers a webhook on your store — every paid order triggers a deduction against your recipe.

02

Place a test order

Place a low-cost test order on your shop and confirm the material quantities deducted match your recipe. Check the deduction log — every entry shows the order ID, timestamp, and materials affected.

03

Cancel your old subscription

Once the test order deducted correctly and your stock levels look right, cancel your old subscription. Keep a local copy of all exports you downloaded before closing the account.
