Detailed notes by competitor

Each tool, stated honestly — including where they are better.

Stocksmith (formerly Craftybase)

Indie: $83–99/mo — $990/yr annual Price concern
Source stocksmith.io/pricing, capterra.com (245 reviews)
Etsy + Shopify Yes, at the Indie tier. Pro ($20/mo) and Studio ($41–49/mo) tiers are not included in the feature comparison matrix on the pricing page; auto-deduction capability at those tiers is undocumented there.
Review record 245 reviews at 4.6/5 on Capterra as of July 2026 (capterra.com). This is the largest independent review record in this product category by a significant margin.
Price history Legacy Craftybase plan with auto-deduction was approximately $19–20/month. The move to Stocksmith Indie at $83–99/month is documented as a roughly 5× increase by named reviewers on Capterra: “I felt like legacy clients had quite a shock when the price went up x5 (literally my price went up that much without exaggeration)” — Raven T., August 8, 2024.
Free trial 14 days (stocksmith.io/pricing). Time-limited trials make it difficult to evaluate whether inventory data is accurate across a real production run.
Where Stocksmith is better than BatchCOGS

245 verified reviews vs. zero for BatchCOGS. Established product with years of real use. Multi-user accounts. Equipment depreciation tracking. Larger feature set at higher tiers. More integration history.

The single deciding factor

If you need auto-deduction on Etsy or Shopify orders, the minimum Stocksmith plan that confirms this feature costs $990/year. BatchCOGS Pro Annual is $180/year for the same feature. The $810/year difference is the only claim BatchCOGS makes against Stocksmith that is grounded solely in verified prices.

Inventora

$23/mo on Shopify App Store — $19/mo on own site (annual) Documented reliability issues
Sources apps.shopify.com/inventora (8 reviews, 3.9/5), inventora.com/pricing-plans, inventora.com/reviews
Pricing inconsistency $23/month on the Shopify App Store (apps.shopify.com/inventora); $19/month annually on their own site (inventora.com/pricing-plans). The App Store is the primary discovery channel for Shopify sellers; the $23/month figure is what most buyers see first.
Documented issues Three categories of problems named by independent App Store reviewers:
1. Overselling during flash sales (“Attaching it to Shopify meant that I oversell my products during flash sales drops”)
2. Inventory duplication on sync (“no clear-cut way to sync between Inventora and Shopify without TONS of duplicating work”)
3. Multi-day support non-response (“it’s days before I get a response”)
Source: Shopify App Store reviews
Free tier Hobby tier: free, 10 orders/month (inventora.com/pricing-plans). BatchCOGS free tier allows 30 orders/month.
Where Inventora is better than BatchCOGS

8 independent reviews (vs. zero for BatchCOGS). Both channels at a lower monthly price than Stocksmith. An established product. Inventora’s bugs may be fixable; BatchCOGS’s reliability is unproven in production.

The core issue

Overselling during a flash sale is a direct business loss. If your Shopify inventory count says you have 50 units and Inventora mis-syncs, you take orders you cannot fill. The 3.9/5 rating on 8 reviews is not a small sample statistical artifact — it is three distinct categories of failure named by real users.

Ardent Seller

$19/mo — free tier: 50 transactions/month Channel limited
Source ardentseller.app
Channels Etsy only. No Shopify integration. If you sell on both platforms, Ardent Seller cannot track your full material usage.
Free tier 50 transactions/month with all features. This is more generous than BatchCOGS free tier (30 orders/month). If you sell only on Etsy and your volume is under 50 orders/month, Ardent Seller’s free tier covers you completely.
Review record Zero independent software reviews on any third-party platform — no Capterra, no G2, no Shopify App Store listing. The “128 reviews” figure cited on the Ardent Seller website refers to the creator’s personal Etsy craft shop (Ardent Workshop), not to software users. There is no independent evidence of reliability or problems.
Where Ardent Seller is better than BatchCOGS

More generous free tier (50 transactions/month vs. 30). Same $19/month paid price. Etsy-focused product may have deeper Etsy-specific features. If you sell only on Etsy and want the free tier, Ardent Seller’s offer is stronger than BatchCOGS’s.

The deciding question

Do you sell on Shopify? If yes, Ardent Seller cannot help you. If you sell only on Etsy and are comfortable with a product that has no independent review record, it is a real option at the same price. BatchCOGS and Ardent Seller are both new and unreviewed — the difference is channel coverage.

FormulaLedger

$19/mo Starter — $39/mo with FDA compliance No channel integration
Source formulaledger.com/pricing
How deduction works You log a production batch manually; stock auto-deducts from that manual entry. The homepage states “Log a batch and stock auto-deducts” — the batch log is a user action, not an event from Etsy or Shopify. There is no Etsy or Shopify integration; orders do not trigger deduction directly.
The practical impact A flash sale of 50 Etsy orders requires you to manually log a batch of 50 to register any deduction. This is the exact workflow the persona is trying to eliminate. FormulaLedger’s BOM engine is genuinely functional; the gap is the missing order-event trigger.
Specialty features FDA MoCRA compliance tracking and INCI ingredient listing — valuable for cosmetics and skincare sellers with regulatory requirements. BatchCOGS does not have these features.
Review record Zero independent reviews. All testimonials are vendor-hosted on the FormulaLedger website.
Where FormulaLedger is better than BatchCOGS

FDA MoCRA compliance features for cosmetics sellers. INCI ingredient tracking. The BOM engine works. For a candle or soap maker with regulatory compliance requirements, FormulaLedger covers ground BatchCOGS does not. The $39/mo Compliance tier may be worth it specifically for that use case.

The threat assessment

FormulaLedger is the highest-threat future competitor. They have the right price, the right persona, and a working BOM engine. If they add Etsy and Shopify webhook integration, the differentiation gap between FormulaLedger and BatchCOGS narrows sharply. The window is estimated at 6–12 months before they could close this gap.

Katana

$199/month and up Out of range for this segment
Source Cited in rescue-etsy-cogs.md research sweep as a product observed during the competitive landscape review, priced above the segment.
Intended buyer Manufacturers with larger production operations, multi-location inventory, and more complex BOM requirements than a handmade seller generating $500–$10,000/month. Not priced for this segment.
Channels Shopify integration. Etsy via third-party integrations — not a direct native connection in the same way as BatchCOGS or Inventora.
Where Katana is better than BatchCOGS

More advanced manufacturing features, multi-location inventory, established product with a verified user base, more robust reporting. If you have outgrown handmade-seller-scale operations, Katana addresses needs BatchCOGS does not. The $199/month starting price reflects that difference.

Spreadsheets (Google Sheets / Excel)

Free No automation
Source User quotes from inventora.com/reviews. Named sellers describe this as their pre-software state.
What they cannot do A spreadsheet cannot receive a webhook from Etsy or Shopify and auto-deduct materials. Every order requires a manual entry. Auto-deduction on order receipt is structurally impossible without an integration.
User-documented pain “challenges with the upkeep of Excel sheets” — Cavelle Wilson (inventora.com/reviews)
“tracking all of my supplies by sight” — Judith Keriopoulos (inventora.com/reviews)
Where spreadsheets are better than BatchCOGS

Free. No vendor dependency — your data is never locked in a third-party system. Fully customizable. For a seller with 5 SKUs and 10 orders a month, a spreadsheet may be sufficient. BatchCOGS asks you to pay $19/month or trust a free-tier new product. A spreadsheet asks nothing.

When to switch

The BatchCOGS free tier is designed to replace a spreadsheet directly: 20 materials, 20 products, 30 orders/month, no credit card. The case for switching is time saved on manual entries and the elimination of the risk of a missed deduction during a high-volume day.

What this comparison shows

BatchCOGS is not better on every dimension. Here is what it does and does not offer.

Where BatchCOGS has an advantage

  • › Price vs. Stocksmith: $180/year vs. $990/year for the same auto-deduction feature, per verified pricing at stocksmith.io/pricing.
  • › Both channels vs. Ardent Seller: Etsy + Shopify vs. Etsy only.
  • › Order-triggered deduction vs. FormulaLedger: auto on order receipt vs. manual batch log.
  • › Free tier order cap vs. Inventora: 30 orders/month free vs. 10 orders/month free.
  • › Idempotency-keyed webhooks: every order deduction is logged with a key that prevents the same order from deducting materials twice, even if the webhook fires more than once.

Where BatchCOGS does not have an advantage

  • – No independent reviews. Stocksmith has 245 Capterra reviews. BatchCOGS has zero. A new product asking you to trust it with core business data has not yet earned that trust.
  • – Free tier order count vs. Ardent Seller: 30 orders/month vs. 50 transactions/month. Ardent Seller’s free tier is more generous if you sell only on Etsy.
  • – No FDA MoCRA compliance features. FormulaLedger has them. If you make cosmetics subject to FDA registration, FormulaLedger’s $39/month Compliance tier addresses a need BatchCOGS does not.
  • – No labor or overhead tracking at launch. Stocksmith tracks more COGS components. BatchCOGS tracks material cost only.
  • – No multi-user accounts at launch. Stocksmith supports them. BatchCOGS does not.
