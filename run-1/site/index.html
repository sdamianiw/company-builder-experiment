How it works

Four things you set up once.

01

Add your materials

Every raw material — beeswax, fragrance oil, wicks, jars, silver wire, resin — with its cost per unit and your current stock quantity. BatchCOGS tracks the cost so you know your actual spend, not an estimate.

02

Build recipes

Link each product SKU to its bill of materials. A 4 oz soy candle uses 3.2 oz wax, 0.4 oz fragrance oil, one wick, one jar. BatchCOGS computes your material cost per unit from those quantities.

03

Connect your shop

Connect Etsy, Shopify, or both. Every order event triggers a deduction. Every deduction is logged with a timestamp and an idempotency key — the same order cannot deduct materials twice, even if the webhook fires more than once.

04

See your COGS

Your material cost per unit, your margin vs. sale price per SKU, your current stock level, and how many units you can still make from what’s on hand — updated after each sale.

Questions

Honest answers.

How is BatchCOGS different from Inventora?

Inventora connects to both Etsy and Shopify and auto-deducts materials on order receipt — the same core feature as BatchCOGS. The documented problem is reliability. Inventora’s Shopify App Store listing shows 8 reviews at 3.9/5 (apps.shopify.com/inventora), with named reviewers confirming: overselling during flash sales, inventory duplication on sync, and multi-day support non-response.

BatchCOGS logs every webhook receipt with an idempotency key — the same order event cannot trigger two deductions. If anything goes wrong, you correct it manually and see the full audit history. Inventora is $23/month on the Shopify App Store; BatchCOGS Pro is $19/month.

How is BatchCOGS different from Ardent Seller?

Ardent Seller (ardentseller.app) is Etsy-only — no Shopify integration. If you sell on both platforms, it cannot track your full inventory. If you sell only on Etsy, it’s a real option at the same $19/month price.

Ardent Seller has zero independent software reviews on any third-party platform (Capterra, G2, Shopify App Store). The “128 reviews” cited on their site come from the creator’s personal Etsy craft shop (Ardent Workshop), not from software users. BatchCOGS aims to build an independent review record from day one.

How is BatchCOGS different from FormulaLedger?

FormulaLedger (formulaledger.com) has a working bill-of-materials engine, but deduction is triggered by manually logging a production batch — not by an incoming order event. Their homepage states “Log a batch and stock auto-deducts” — the batch log is a manual step the user takes.

FormulaLedger also has no Etsy or Shopify integration. A seller receiving 50 Etsy orders during a flash sale would need to manually log a batch of 50 to trigger any deduction. BatchCOGS deducts the moment the order lands, with no manual step in between. FormulaLedger is the highest-threat future competitor if they add sales channel integration.

Is the free tier really free, with no time limit?

Yes. No credit card required, no 14-day trial countdown. The free tier has hard limits — 20 materials, 20 products, 1 channel, 30 orders/month — so you can verify that auto-deduction works correctly before paying anything.

An active seller hitting 30+ orders a month on Etsy will outgrow the free tier within weeks. The limits exist so the free tier demonstrates reliability, not so it disappears before you’ve had time to evaluate it. Stocksmith’s free trial is 14 days (stocksmith.io/pricing) — too short to trust core business data to a new tool.

What happens if a webhook fails?

Every webhook receipt is logged. If a webhook fails, no materials are deducted — nothing happens silently. You see the failed event in your sync log with the specific Etsy or Shopify error code. You can retry the deduction or apply it manually. Every manual correction is logged with a timestamp. The sync dashboard shows the last successful sync time and any queued or failed events.

Does BatchCOGS track labor or overhead, or only materials?

At launch, BatchCOGS tracks material costs only — the raw inputs in your recipe. Labor and overhead are not in the current version. You can add them as a manual line on the COGS view if you want an approximate fully-loaded cost, but they are not auto-calculated. This is not a limitation we’re hiding; it’s the honest scope of the MVP.
