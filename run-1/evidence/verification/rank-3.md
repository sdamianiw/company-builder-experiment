## 3. Recency Assessment — Is the Pain Current?

**Partially yes, but with significant nuance.**

- Active Teachers Pay Teachers market for Google Sheets IEP trackers (updated for 2025–2026 and 2026–2027 school years) confirms teachers are still using spreadsheet workarounds. TPT products with auto-graphing formulas are selling well in 2025–2026.
- Edutopia published content on reducing IEP paperwork burden using Google Forms in recent years, suggesting mainstream educational media still treats this as an unsolved problem — which indicates adoption of dedicated tools is incomplete.
- However, the fact that AbleSpace has 10,000+ users and free/low-cost pricing means the "no solution" framing is outdated or simply incorrect. The market is partially served.
- The pain of paperwork burden is real and current. The claim that it is *unserved* by software is stale.

## 4. Buildability Assessment — One Person, One Month

**Not meaningfully buildable.** Multiple factors:

1. **AbleSpace already exists with a free tier.** A new entrant would need to match or exceed a free product on UX, trust, and compliance from day one. That bar is not achievable in one month.
2. **Compliance requirements are substantial.** HIPAA and FERPA compliance (mandatory for handling student disability data) requires legal review, infrastructure configuration, and security audits. These cannot be shortcut in a month.
3. **Network effects.** Paraprofessionals need to share data with teachers who share it with case managers. A new app requires enough users in the same school to be useful. Solo launch cannot address this.
4. **Trust barrier.** Special education involves sensitive student data. Schools and teachers will not adopt new software from an unknown provider without vetting, especially post-FERPA enforcement actions.
5. **What is theoretically buildable:** A niche version (e.g., targeting one specific state's IEP format, or one type of data collection method) could produce an MVP in a month. But "meaningful software" for the broad market described is not achievable.

## 6. Final Verdict

**REFUTED.**

The underlying pain (paperwork burden, compliance-centric software, para data logging friction) is real and current. But the central differentiating claim — that no lightweight, paraprofessional-friendly mobile app exists at a teacher-payable price — is false. AbleSpace is exactly that product: free-to-start, $18/month paid, mobile app on iOS/Android, explicitly paraprofessional-friendly, and serving 10,000+ special ed professionals. A solo builder entering this market in one month would compete against an established, trusted, free incumbent with compliance certifications and network effects.

The evidence URLs are also weak: one is a vendor marketing blog, the other is an unverified replit-hosted site with no independent authority. The pain is real but the opportunity gap claimed here does not exist at the level described.
