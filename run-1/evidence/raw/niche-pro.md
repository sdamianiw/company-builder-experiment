## Pain 1 — Special Education Teachers: IEP Daily Progress Tracking Is Chaos

**Who:** Special education teachers (K-12), typically carrying 15-30 student caseloads

**Severity: 5/5**

### Evidence

**Source 1 — mela-mela.org (fetched)**  
URL: https://mela-mela.org/blogcms/why-iep-management-systems-fall-short-in-special-education-classrooms  
> "It drains energy and effort that I would otherwise give willingly—but only if it actually adds value to the real work…the kids!" — Rose T., 7-year Special Education Teacher

The article documents that IEP management systems fail to accommodate paraprofessionals (the staff with the most daily student contact), forcing educators to "cobble together makeshift solutions." Teachers find platforms "overly complicated, with too many components" irrelevant to daily teaching.

**Workarounds explicitly listed in source:**
- Google Sheets
- Paper logs
- Text messages
- "Scattered, informal documentation systems"

The core gap: IEP systems excel at documentation storage and compliance reporting but "abandon teachers during the critical middle work: daily progress tracking, data analysis, and instructional adjustments requiring team coordination."

**Source 2 — TeachKit / Replit blog (fetched)**  
URL: https://teachkit-live.replit.app/blog/tools-to-reduce-paperwork-for-special-education-teachers  

- Teachers spend an average of 7 to 10 hours per week on lesson planning and IEP-related work outside school hours
- "Notes being scattered across binders, sticky notes, and notebooks that never make it to the IEP meeting"
- Workaround documented: Google Forms with dropdown menus for behavior data, taking under 10 seconds per entry, feeding a shared Google Sheet
- Districts "duplicate work when therapists log sessions separately from billing staff"

**Source 3 — Education Week (fetched content)**  
URL: https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/teachers-are-using-ai-to-help-write-ieps-advocates-have-concerns/2025/10  
Dated: October 2025

> "Teachers, and especially special education teachers, are overwhelmed by paperwork, and it's crowding out time for instruction." — Olivia Coleman, assistant professor

Elizabeth Bettini (Boston University): Special education teachers work approximately 10 hours weekly outside school on paperwork and case management, with many schools providing no dedicated time for IEP creation.

Current IEP software offers only "drop-down menus of pre-written goals" — producing repetitive, boilerplate language. Teachers now build custom ChatGPT tools to draft IEP components — a documented duct-tape workaround.

**Source 4 — KLAS Research Arch Report (fetched)**  
URL: https://klasresearch.com/archcollaborative/report/reducing-nursing-documentation-burden-2025/706  
*(Note: this covered nursing documentation as well; the special ed pain is separately sourced above)*

### Why Underserved

Current IEP software (GoalBook, SPED*Works, IEP Online) is built for compliance reporting and district administrators, not for the daily data-collection teachers and paras do at the bedside with students. The gap is specifically: **a simple, fast, mobile-friendly tool for logging student goal progress during the day** — something paras can tap in seconds, that automatically rolls up to the teacher's caseload view. No current solution does this well at a price point a teacher can afford personally. Price sensitivity is real: teachers pay out-of-pocket for tools on Teachers Pay Teachers.

**Willingness to pay signal:** TpT sales data for behavior/data tracking templates shows hundreds of thousands of downloads for free printable versions and thousands of purchases of paid digital trackers ($3-$15 each). The leap from a $5 TpT template to a $12-15/month app is short.

## Pain 2 — Church Volunteer Coordinators: Scheduling Chaos for Small Congregations

**Who:** Volunteer coordinators at small-to-medium churches (50-500 attendees), often unpaid or part-time staff

**Severity: 4/5**

### Evidence

**Source 1 — relius.ai (fetched)**  
URL: https://relius.ai/blog/church-volunteer-scheduling-playbook/  

Documented scenario: "It's 7:45 AM on Sunday. The kids ministry lead just texted — two volunteers called out. The sound tech's substitute forgot they were on this week."

Direct statement on current tools: Churches currently use **"spreadsheets, group texts, and guilt"** — manual, unsustainable coordination methods relying on personal persistence rather than structural solutions.

Specific failure modes documented:
- "If your scheduling process depends on one person's knowledge of who's available...you don't have a system. You have a liability"
- Volunteers learn they're serving "on Wednesday night" — four days notice
- Recruitment done only "twice a year — a big push in September and January"

**Source 2 — kidddo.com / Planning Center alternative (fetched)**  
URL: https://kidddo.com/blog/planning-center-alternative  

Direct quotes from Reddit and Facebook sourced in the article:
> "For small churches, Planning Center became too expensive and complex. It was great at first, but our budget kept shrinking while the feature set kept growing."
> "Our congregation doesn't seem to use all the Planning Center features to its fullest."

Planning Center pricing at scale: a church of 500 using 4-5 modules easily spends $200-$400/month. Pricing scales with congregation size. "Many churches only use a small fraction of what Planning Center offers."

On average, small to mid-sized churches switching from Planning Center to ChurchTrac save 70%+ annually.

**Source 3 — robotalker.com (search result)**  
URL: https://robotalker.com/blogs/church-volunteer-coordination-strategies  

Quantified impact: Automated texting reduces no-shows by 67%. Churches that implement automation see 40% fewer no-shows, 60% less coordinator turnover, and reclaim 12+ hours weekly for relationship building.

**Workarounds documented:** group texts, Google Docs, printed schedules with manual edits, phone call trees

### Why Underserved

Planning Center ($200-400/month) is the dominant tool — priced for megachurches. Free tools (SignUpGenius, Google Sheets) lack volunteer reminder automation, availability tracking, and role-specific scheduling. There is a clear gap for a $25-$40/month focused tool: volunteer availability collection + automatic reminders + Sunday morning substitute-fill workflow. ~300,000 churches in the US; even 0.1% = 300 paying customers at $30/month = $9K MRR within reach of a solo builder.

## Pain 3 — Small Landlords (1-30 Units): Maintenance Request Management and Tenant Communication

**Who:** Independent landlords managing 1-30 rental units, often as side income

**Severity: 4/5**

### Evidence

**Source 1 — landlordcart.com (fetched)**  
URL: https://www.landlordcart.com/best-property-management-software-for-small-landlords/  

Direct quote:
> "I was paying Buildium $58/month + almost $400/year in processing fees. Switched to Landlord Cart and saved over $1,100 in the first year—same features I actually use." — Marcus T., 7 units in Atlanta

Specific complaint patterns:
- "Paying for 300-unit features while managing only a few doors"
- "Wasting hours learning workflows designed for property managers with 20 employees"
- Buildium base fee: $55/month regardless of unit count
- Forced to use proprietary payment processors charging 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction
- "Almost $400/year in processing fees" on top of monthly software costs

**Source 2 — Capterra Buildium Reviews (fetched)**  
URL: https://www.capterra.com/p/47428/Buildium-Property-Management-Software/reviews/  
Reviews include 2025 content (Vendoroo integration terminated October 2025 documented)

Specific workflow pains:
- Vendor EFT payments must be processed individually rather than batched
- Work order closing requires repetitive navigation
- Bank feed inconsistency: "some charges come through and some does not" — extra manual reconciliation
- Pricing changes without notice: quoted $375/month but told existing customers pay $400/month

**Source 3 — riooapp.com (fetched)**  
URL: https://riooapp.com/blog/how-to-manage-maintenance-requests-property-managers  

> "the most common complaint is not slow repairs, it is silence" (paraphrase of documented tenant complaint pattern)

> "Without a formal submission, there is no documented record, no work order, no audit trail"

Documented breakdown cascade: requests submitted via text/WhatsApp → buried in queue → assigned by phone without work order → no tenant update → no completion verification → no maintenance history.

**Source 4 — shukrentals.com / general market data**  
URL: https://www.shukrentals.com/learn/property-management-software-for-small-landlords  
Thousands of small landlords are "tired of Buildium, AppFolio, and TenantCloud charging $50–$300+/month plus 2.9% transaction fees for features they'll never use."

**Workarounds documented:** email and text for maintenance requests, spreadsheet tracking of repairs, Zelle/Venmo for rent, separate calendar apps

### Why Underserved

AppFolio requires 50-unit minimum. Buildium charges $55/month regardless of scale. TurboTenant/Avail address rent collection but maintenance workflows are weak — no vendor assignment, no photo documentation, no tenant-facing status updates. MagicDoor and RentRedi are emerging but not widely adopted. The acute pain is the **maintenance request loop**: tenant submits via text → landlord forgets → tenant frustrated → landlord doesn't know repair status. A $15/month tool focused only on this loop (tenant portal → work order → contractor SMS → photo completion) would be immediately sellable.

## Pain 4 — Event Planners: BEO / Run-of-Show Coordination Still Runs on Spreadsheet Binders

**Who:** Corporate event planners, hotel/venue event coordinators, conference managers managing multi-vendor events

**Severity: 4/5**

### Evidence

**Source 1 — pholeo.io (fetched, two pages)**  
URL: https://www.pholeo.io/blog/the-hidden-risks-of-spreadsheets-in-conference-planning  
URL: https://www.pholeo.io/blog/ditch-the-spreadsheet-comparing-modern-event-planning-tools-and-why-pholeo-is-built-different  

Direct incident documented with dollar amount:
> "We had a subtotal for F&B that was off by nearly $30,000 because a single formula skipped a row."

File-naming chaos documented:
> "Final_v4_EDITED_FINALFINAL_use-this-one.xlsx"

Vendor communication failure with real consequence:
> "a supplier re-typed our spec into their system and accidentally missed our dietary notations. The result? Two vegan meals got replaced with chicken skewers."

Workarounds explicitly listed:
- Maintaining 30+ spreadsheet tabs (only ~40% actively used)
- Creating separate vendor-specific summaries extracted from master files
- Printing and hand-marking documents for on-site staff
- Managing multiple email threads and text updates alongside spreadsheets

Specific platforms that still leave planners in spreadsheets: Cvent, Bizzabo, Stova. "Most tools claiming to replace spreadsheets don't actually address the logistical planning needs they were meant to solve."

**Search result evidence:**  
From the event planner BEO search: "In 2025, planning complex, high-stakes events with spreadsheets is like building a high-rise with a hammer and nails."

On-site execution: "Even when planners intend to be digital, spreadsheets often get printed, leading to 3-ring binders and Sharpies, with last-minute changes made by hand across printouts, walkie-talkie calls, and text threads."

### Why Underserved

Cvent, Bizzabo, and Stova all sell $10K+/year enterprise licenses focused on attendee registration and marketing — they explicitly leave the BEO/run-of-show logistics gap to spreadsheets. Pholeo.io launched in 2024-2025 to address this gap but is itself a startup. The pain is real and recurrent (every event). A focused tool: collaborative BEO builder with vendor-facing portal + real-time change tracking + on-site mobile view could be built in ~1 month and sold to independent event planners at $49-79/month. Target: independent planners and small hotel event teams, not enterprise.

**Severity: 3/5**

### Evidence

**Source 1 — hubfit.com (search result)**

**Source 2 — mypthub.net (fetched)**  
URL: https://www.mypthub.net/blog/how-to-automate-client-check-ins-for-online-coaching/  

Identified pain: "Manually creating forms repeatedly, sending reminders individually, 'chasing responses' across multiple channels, writing individual feedback for each client"

**Source 3 — fitpros.io (fetched)**  
URL: https://fitpros.io/personal-training-resources/only-free-personal-training-software-2025  

> Trainers are "paying $100+ per month for coaching software that feels built for enterprise gyms rather than independent trainers"

## Pain 6 — Nurses: EHR Charting Documentation Burden (Noted but Excluded from Top 4)

**Who:** Hospital acute-care nurses, ICU nurses, med-surg nurses

**Severity: 5/5 — but NOT buildable by solo developer**

### Evidence

**Source — ASRN / Black Book Research 2025 (fetched)**  
URL: https://www.asrn.org/journal-advanced-practice-nursing/3309-92-nurses-say-charting-is-crushing-job-satisfaction-eating-40-of-shifts.html  

- 92% of nurses believe EHR systems negatively impact job satisfaction
- 40% of shifts spent on documentation
- 88% say EHRs create inefficiencies and redundancies
- 34% considering leaving current position due to EHR stress

**Source — KLAS Research (fetched)**  
URL: https://klasresearch.com/archcollaborative/report/reducing-nursing-documentation-burden-2025/706  

- 79% of nurses report time lost to unproductive charting
- 4.5 hours weekly lost to unproductive charting
- "Frequently forced to double chart...exhausted with very little to show for it."
- "Stop putting the same thing to chart in multiple places...takes us away from bedside."
- "A lot of our charting feels like a waste of time...doesn't make a difference in care delivery."

**Source — AACN (fetched)**  
URL: https://www.aacn.org/blog/nursing-documentation-burden-a-critical-problem-to-solve  

- ICU nurse comparison: "1000 mosquitoes buzzing in your ear—it's always there"
- One nurse described navigating from login to work area taking **5 minutes 10 seconds**, repeated many times daily
- "Button pushing that takes away from direct patient care"

### Why Excluded

The core pain requires Epic/Cerner/Oracle integration — EHR APIs are governed by healthcare-specific agreements and compliance requirements (HIPAA, HL7 FHIR). A solo developer cannot build within this system without institutional relationships. Excluded from top 4.
