BatchCOGS — the whole package in five minutes

Recipe-based COGS and inventory tracking for handmade sellers on Etsy and Shopify, at $19/month. This page links every deliverable and states plainly what is verified, what is inference, and what the red team could not resolve.

1. The pain (verified, primary sources)

Handmade sellers need materials to auto-deduct when a sale comes in, and real per-unit COGS for pricing and taxes. The segment leader, Craftybase — rebranded Stocksmith in July 2026 — gates that feature ("Automated production assemblies") at its Indie tier, $83–99/month (stocksmith.io/pricing, re-verified live 2026-07-24). Cheaper Stocksmith tiers exist (Pro $20, Studio $41–49) but are not documented to include it.

"Having materials automatically deduct when I sell a product is very basic functionality that I should not have to pay $1,000 / year to have" — Liza K., Capterra review of Craftybase/Stocksmith
"I felt like legacy clients had quite a shock when the price went up x5 (literally my price went up that much)" — Raven T., same source (quoted as the reviewer's own experience, not our figure)

The sub-$25 alternatives all exist and all fail this persona in a documented way: Inventora ($23/mo) has Shopify App Store reviews reporting sync duplication and overselling during flash sales; Ardent Seller ($19/mo) is Etsy-only with zero independent software reviews; FormulaLedger ($19/mo) has no sales-channel integration. Full adjudication: narrowed re-verification and external push-back audit.

4. The business case, honestly framed

Thesis · competitor map · winning tournament pitch. Market context: Etsy reports 5.6M marketplace active sellers as of Dec 31, 2024 (Etsy 10-K, SEC). The share who self-make from raw materials is unverified, so all TAM math is labeled inference on top of that unknown. The realistic 18-month outcome stated in the thesis is 500–1,000 paying users ($114k–$228k ARR) — inference, not forecast.

Why now: the July 2026 rebrand created a displacement window for auto-deduction users; the honest moat assessment is thin and time-bounded (12–24 months) — reliability reputation + data switching cost + migration tooling, earned not assumed.

5. The red team tried to kill it

red-team.md: 22 objections across business, product, and economics lenses — verbatim, with honest responses. 2 answered, 4 mitigated, 16 open. The open ones are open on purpose; the sharpest:

  • Multiple young entrants (FormulaLedger, Batchforja, Ardent Seller) are attacking this same wedge now; the moat window may be shorter than 12 months.
  • A segment defined by refusing $49/month may cap willingness-to-pay near $9–15, not $19.
  • Etsy Commercial Access is a manual approval gate — apply on build day one; CSV import is the fallback (Etsy docs).
  • Whether Stocksmith Studio ($41–49) quietly includes auto-deduction is the largest unresolved factual question — a trial account test resolves it before launch.
  • Seasonal churn (Q4 peak → January cancellations) and a Germany-based founder serving a US-centric segment are structural frictions.

Two red-team findings were defects in this package itself and were fixed, not filed: the switching kit now states plainly that one-click CSV import ships in the first 60 days (not day one), and its field map now describes ID-based (not title-based) product linking.
